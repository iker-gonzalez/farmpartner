● OUR LIMITS

What the data can’t see.

Honest advice starts with being honest about its own gaps. These are the known limits of the current version of FarmPartner. The bot says them out loud in its answers too, so every farmer knows how far to trust each recommendation.

  1. 01AGRONOMY

    Crop rules are a first approximation.

    Crop traits and scoring weights are hackathon approximations from public extension guidance. They need agronomist review. New combinations from the rotation search are less proven: try them on part of a field first.

    WHAT IT MEANS

    How much heat, water or frost each crop tolerates, and how much each factor weighs in the score, comes from general public guidance, not from a study of your region.

    FOR YOU

    Use the scores to compare options and start a conversation, not as a final verdict. If something doesn’t match your experience, trust your experience and tell the bot.

    CLOSING THE GAP

    Review by agronomists, validation with farmers in real fields, and the know-how of experienced farmers feeding the knowledge base the AI consults.

  2. 02RESOLUTION

    Satellites see in blocks, not in fields.

    POWER is a ~50 km model grid, and a MODIS pixel (250 m) can mix fields. CDL can mislabel some years. GRACE is a ~300 km signal: the regional aquifer trend, not one well.

    WHAT IT MEANS

    NASA POWER climate describes a square about 50 km wide. One MODIS vegetation pixel covers 250 × 250 m, so a small or narrow field can be blended with its neighbors. The USDA crop map (CDL) is itself made from satellites and sometimes gets a year wrong. GRACE groundwater trends describe a region about 300 km across.

    FOR YOU

    Climate reads describe your area rather than your exact plot. Microclimates such as valleys, slopes or frost pockets may not show. If the crop history looks wrong, correct it in the chat.

    CLOSING THE GAP

    Finer-resolution sources such as Landsat (30 m) for vegetation, plus the farmer’s own corrections, which always take priority over the satellite reading.

  3. 03PRICES & YIELDS

    No local prices yet.

    No local prices yet. “Income” is a relative index plus the trial-based yield effect, and the bot asks farmers for local prices and buyers.

    WHAT IT MEANS

    FarmPartner doesn’t know your market prices or your own yields. The “income” score ranks rotations against each other, using a relative market index and the yield gain that 3,663 field trials show after each previous crop (Mudare et al. 2025); it is not a forecast in dollars or euros.

    FOR YOU

    Before switching crops, check local prices, buyers and contracts. The bot asks you about them so the comparison reflects your real market.

    CLOSING THE GAP

    Connecting local price and yield data, and learning from the prices and buyers farmers share in the conversation.

  4. 04COVERAGE

    Deepest in the U.S., useful worldwide.

    U.S.-deepest: CDL and SSURGO are U.S.-only. Climate, NDVI and the forecast work worldwide; outside the U.S. the farmer types their crops and FAOSTAT limits the search to crops the country grows.

    WHAT IT MEANS

    Crop history (USDA CDL) and detailed soil data (USDA SSURGO) only exist for the United States. NASA climate, vegetation health (NDVI), soil moisture, crop stress, groundwater trends and the weather forecast cover the whole planet.

    FOR YOU

    Outside the U.S., tell the bot which crops you grow and what your soil is like. The climate analysis works just the same; crop history and soil detail come from you.

    CLOSING THE GAP

    Global soil and crop-type datasets, so farmers everywhere get the same depth of analysis without typing it in.

  5. 05SATELLITE TIMING

    Some satellites answer minutes later, and not every day.

    SMAP is a 9 km grid. ECOSTRESS passes are irregular, and clouds leave gaps. Both arrive 20–30 minutes after onboarding, and until then the report lists them as “processing”.

    WHAT IT MEANS

    SMAP (soil moisture) and ECOSTRESS (crop water stress) are requested from NASA AppEEARS for each new field. NASA processes them as a queue, so they come in 20–30 minutes after the first answer. SMAP describes a 9 km square; ECOSTRESS sees 70 m but only on the days the space station passes over, and clouds hide some of those passes.

    FOR YOU

    Your first comparison uses climate, vegetation and crop maps; soil moisture and crop stress are added to the report and to the watering advice once they arrive. If your soil is wetter or drier than the bot thinks, say so.

    CLOSING THE GAP

    Requesting the satellite data ahead of time for common farming regions, and adding finer, more frequent soil-moisture products as they become available.

■ WHY WE SAY THIS

A recommendation is only useful if you know how far to trust it. That’s why every answer comes with its evidence, the data gaps and the risks to check locally.
