● OUR LIMITS

What the data can’t see.

Honest advice starts with being honest about its own gaps. These are the known limits of the current version of FarmPartner. The bot says them out loud in its answers too, so every farmer knows how far to trust each recommendation.

  • 01AGRONOMY
  • 02RESOLUTION
  • 03PRICES & YIELDS
  • 04COVERAGE
  • 05MISSING DATA

  1. 01AGRONOMY

    Crop rules are a first approximation.

    Crop traits and scoring weights are hackathon approximations from public extension guidance. They need agronomist review.

    WHAT IT MEANS

    How much heat, water or frost each crop tolerates, and how much each factor weighs in the score, comes from general public guidance, not from a study of your region.

    FOR YOU

    Use the scores to compare options and start a conversation, not as a final verdict. If something doesn’t match your experience, trust your experience and tell the bot.

    CLOSING THE GAP

    Review by agronomists, validation with farmers in real fields, and the know-how of experienced farmers feeding the knowledge base the AI consults.

  2. 02RESOLUTION

    Satellites see in blocks, not in fields.

    POWER is a ~50 km model grid, and a MODIS pixel (250 m) can mix fields. CDL can mislabel some years.

    WHAT IT MEANS

    NASA POWER climate describes a square about 50 km wide. One MODIS vegetation pixel covers 250 × 250 m, so a small or narrow field can be blended with its neighbors. The USDA crop map (CDL) is itself made from satellites and sometimes gets a year wrong.

    FOR YOU

    Climate reads describe your area rather than your exact plot. Microclimates such as valleys, slopes or frost pockets may not show. If the crop history looks wrong, correct it in the chat.

    CLOSING THE GAP

    Finer-resolution sources such as Landsat (30 m) for vegetation, plus the farmer’s own corrections, which always take priority over the satellite reading.

  3. 03PRICES & YIELDS

    No prices or yields yet.

    No prices or yields yet. “Income” is a relative index, and the bot asks farmers for local prices and buyers.

    WHAT IT MEANS

    FarmPartner doesn’t know market prices or expected yields. The “income” score only ranks rotations against each other; it is not a forecast in dollars or euros.

    FOR YOU

    Before switching crops, check local prices, buyers and contracts. The bot asks you about them so the comparison reflects your real market.

    CLOSING THE GAP

    Connecting local price and yield data, and learning from the prices and buyers farmers share in the conversation.

  4. 04COVERAGE

    Deepest in the U.S., useful worldwide.

    U.S.-deepest: CDL and SSURGO are U.S.-only. Climate, NDVI and the forecast work worldwide, and outside the U.S. the farmer types their crops.

    WHAT IT MEANS

    Crop history (USDA CDL) and detailed soil data (USDA SSURGO) only exist for the United States. NASA climate, vegetation health (NDVI) and the weather forecast cover the whole planet.

    FOR YOU

    Outside the U.S., tell the bot which crops you grow and what your soil is like. The climate analysis works just the same; crop history and soil detail come from you.

    CLOSING THE GAP

    Global soil and crop-type datasets, so farmers everywhere get the same depth of analysis without typing it in.

  5. 05MISSING DATA

    Two NASA datasets aren’t wired in yet.

    SMAP (9 km soil moisture) and ECOSTRESS (ET) need Earthdata login and are not wired in yet. POWER’s GWETROOT stands in for soil moisture.

    WHAT IT MEANS

    SMAP measures soil moisture from space and ECOSTRESS measures how much water plants release (evapotranspiration). Both need a NASA Earthdata account, so for now soil moisture comes from POWER’s modeled root-zone wetness (GWETROOT).

    FOR YOU

    Soil-moisture reads are a modeled, coarser estimate rather than a direct measurement. If your soil is wetter or drier than the bot thinks, say so.

    CLOSING THE GAP

    Connecting SMAP and ECOSTRESS through NASA Earthdata for measured soil moisture and plant water use.

■ WHY WE SAY THIS

A recommendation is only useful if you know how far to trust it. That’s why every answer comes with its evidence, a confidence level and the risks to check locally.
